How to Compress Images Without Losing Quality

📅 2026⏱ 5 min read✍️ Mian Hassam

Image compression is one of the most impactful things you can do to speed up a website or reduce storage. The challenge is doing it without introducing visible quality loss.

💡 Quick tip: Most users cannot tell the difference between a JPG at quality 85 and the original. WebP at quality 80 is typically 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality.

Understanding Lossy vs Lossless Compression

Lossy compression (JPG, WebP) removes some image data to reduce file size. Done well, the quality loss is invisible to the human eye.

Lossless compression (PNG, WebP lossless) preserves every pixel exactly. File sizes are larger but quality is perfect — ideal for logos, icons, and text-heavy screenshots.

The Best Quality Settings

  • Quality 90–100% — Visually identical to original, minimal savings (5–15%)
  • Quality 75–85% — The sweet spot: invisible quality loss, 40–60% savings
  • Quality 60–75% — Noticeable on close inspection, 60–75% savings
  • Quality below 60% — Visible artifacts, only for non-critical thumbnails

Why WebP Is Almost Always the Best Choice

WebP, developed by Google, offers the best balance of quality and file size. At equivalent quality settings, WebP files are typically:

  • 25–35% smaller than JPEG for photographs
  • 25% smaller than PNG for graphics with transparency
  • Supported by Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari 14+, and all modern mobile browsers

When to Use Each Format

  • JPG — Photographs, social media posts, email attachments, maximum compatibility
  • PNG — Logos, icons, screenshots with text, images requiring transparency
  • WebP — Everything on modern websites where you want the smallest file size
