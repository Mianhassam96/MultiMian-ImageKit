How to Compress Images Without Losing Quality

📅 Updated Nov 2026⏱ 6 min read✍️ Mian Hassam

Large uncompressed images are the single biggest cause of slow web page loading and wasted storage. According to HTTP Archive data, images account for over 50% of the total page weight on typical websites. Compressing your images can slash bandwidth usage by 70% to 90% without causing noticeable visual degradation.

💡 Key Takeaway: Setting lossy compression to 80–82% quality in WebP format delivers virtually indistinguishable visual fidelity while reducing file sizes by up to 85% compared to raw camera exports.

Lossy vs. Lossless: Which Should You Use?

Image compression algorithms fall into two fundamental categories:

  • Lossy Compression (JPG, WebP, AVIF): Selectively discards subtle color frequencies and high-frequency noise that the human eye cannot easily distinguish. This produces massive file size drops. Ideal for photos, backgrounds, and hero art.
  • Lossless Compression (PNG, WebP Lossless): Re-encodes image data with mathematical precision using algorithms like DEFLATE. Every single pixel remains 100% identical. Ideal for logos, icons, technical diagrams, and crisp text screenshots.

Three Mistakes to Avoid When Compressing

  1. Compressing Already-Compressed Files Repeatedly: Re-saving a low-quality JPG multiple times introduces compounding compression artifacts (banding and edge ringing). Always keep your original source file and compress directly from it.
  2. Serving Full 4K Resolution on Mobile: Serving a 4000px wide image to a 390px phone display wastes megabytes of bandwidth. Resize the dimensions first, then apply compression.
  3. Ignoring WebP: Sticking exclusively to legacy JPG leaves 25% to 35% of potential size savings on the table. WebP is now supported by 96%+ of all browsers worldwide.
