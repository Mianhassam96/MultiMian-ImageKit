How to Merge Multiple Images into One (Side-by-Side or Stacked)

📅 Nov 2026⏱ 5 min read✍️ Mian Hassam

Combining multiple images into a single file is common across social media, client reporting, real estate listings, and design work. Whether you are assembling a side-by-side before-and-after comparison, a multi-panel tutorial strip, or compiling receipts into a PDF, stitching images shouldn't require installing bulky desktop software or uploading personal files to random cloud servers.

🔒 Privacy First: MultiMian ImageKit processes and combines your images 100% on your device using the HTML5 Canvas API. Your photos never leave your computer or phone.

Choosing the Right Layout

Depending on your goal, you will typically want one of two layouts:

  • Horizontal (Side-by-Side): Ideal for two pictures showcasing before/after comparisons, product variations, or panoramic photo pairs. Matches landscape screens and desktop presentations.
  • Vertical (Stacked): Ideal for step-by-step tutorials, comic strips, mobile infographics, or compiling multiple receipts into a single scrollable image or document.

How to Align Images with Different Aspect Ratios

A common problem when combining pictures is handling different source resolutions (for example, joining a 1920×1080 horizontal photo with a 1080×1920 vertical portrait). When stitching:

  • For Side-by-Side: Scale all images to match the height of the tallest image while maintaining each photo's proportional width.
  • For Stacked: Scale all images to match the width of the widest image while maintaining proportional height.

MultiMian ImageKit handles proportional scaling automatically so your combined output looks clean and intentional without distorted stretching.

Exporting as Image vs. PDF

Export Format Best For Advantages
PNG Graphic design, logos, screenshots Lossless sharpness, perfectly crisp borders and text.
JPG Photographs, social sharing Compact file size, easy to attach to emails and message threads.
PDF Contracts, receipts, multi-page docs Universal document printing, vector text preservation via jsPDF.
